# Matchmaker assistant and the next product opportunities

Research date: September 25, 2026. The assistant is implemented; the additional ideas below are proposals, not built features.

## The problem to solve

A matchmaker has to piece together stated requirements, profile facts, past decisions, and unresolved questions before deciding whom to introduce. A useful assistant should reduce that reconstruction work while keeping eligibility and evidence visible. It should not simply produce a confident-sounding compatibility score.

## Implemented assistant

Open **AI assistant** in the sidebar or **Ask your matchmaker assistant** in Match queue. Choose a client, then prepare a brief, find unintroduced profiles to review, inspect unknowns, or summarize reviewed feedback. With a Gemini key configured, the same workspace supports free-form questions and follow-up questions.

Each request reloads the selected client's records. Deterministic ranking scans the active pool. The language model receives the first eight available ranked profiles, up to six named profiles mentioned in current/recent questions, up to three blocked examples, the last eight reviewed feedback records, up to five signals, and stated preferences. It is not a full-history search engine. Suggested cards can only contain candidates in the first eight available results; cards are displayed in deterministic ranking order. Existing introductions, client hard-rule conflicts and known reciprocal conflicts are excluded from new recommendations. Missing reciprocal information is visibly incomplete.

Answers include expandable recorded evidence and server-built navigation links. The server rejects invented evidence IDs and ineligible/unavailable recommendation IDs. These checks do not prove every sentence generated by a model: the matchmaker must still inspect its interpretation. The assistant has no database-writing tools and cannot send messages, edit preferences or schedule a meeting. It directs users into existing reviewed workflows. History is limited to this page session, resets with client changes, and is not stored in the database. At most six prior questions are supplied to Gemini; previous AI answers are not treated as evidence.

Without a key, the four guided tasks work using deterministic records, explicitly labeled **Guided summary**. Free-form questions explain the limitation. Provider failures preserve the question and offer retry or guided summary. No live Gemini generation is claimed until tested with a configured key.

## New ideas worth testing

| Priority | Idea | Real problem | Small MVP | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clarification interview coach | Broad words such as “modern” or “family-oriented” conceal different meanings. | From recorded unknowns, suggest three concrete questions for a client call. Store the client's answers separately; a human approves any preference update. | Unknown requirements resolved per call; subsequent rejections tied to already-known requirements. |
| 2 | Readiness and freshness check | A suitable profile may no longer be available or open to meeting. | Add last-confirmed timestamps and an explicit expiry policy for availability, city and introduction consent. Route stale records to reconfirmation, not automatic introductions. | Introductions cancelled because of stale facts; proportion of active profiles reconfirmed. |
| 3 | Candidate supply-gap map | The bottleneck may be the available pool rather than the ranking algorithm. | For clients with few options, show counts eliminated by each hard requirement and combinations. Generate a human-reviewed recruiting brief without relaxing deal-breakers. | Clients with no eligible options; time to recruit a qualifying profile. |
| 4 | Small-batch introduction planner | Repeatedly sending many profiles can create a tiring review experience. | Help a matchmaker select a small, deliberate batch of eligible profiles with different relevant strengths and review the client's response before sending more. Start with a configurable batch, not a universal “three is best” rule. | Review completion, response time and client satisfaction, alongside acceptance. |
| 5 | Private post-meeting debrief | Profile acceptance does not explain whether an actual meeting went well. | Ask each person independently about comfort, conversation and willingness to meet again. Keep private answers separate; only share information explicitly approved for sharing. | Debrief completion; mutual second-meeting interest; reasons for discontinuing. |
| 6 | Matchmaker handover brief | Context gets lost when another team member takes over a client. | Produce a dated, editable brief with confirmed requirements, decisions, outstanding questions and source links. Separate facts from hypotheses and record reviewer approval. | Handover preparation time; repeated questions; missed follow-ups. |

For the next iteration, prioritize the clarification coach and freshness check. They address incomplete or outdated inputs, which more elaborate scoring cannot repair. This prioritization is a product inference; validate it in interviews with working matchmakers.

## What the research supports

- [Nielsen Norman Group, AI chatbot design guidelines (2026)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) reports usability research supporting clear capabilities, context awareness, suggested prompt buttons, visible recommendation details, and concise expandable answers. These informed the assistant UI. It does not demonstrate an improvement in matchmaking outcomes.
- [Microsoft Research, effective human–AI interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/how-to-build-effective-human-ai-interaction-considerations-for-machine-learning-and-software-engineering/) discusses validated human–AI design guidelines and the need to account for errors and user control. This supports inspectable evidence and explicit human decisions rather than opaque automatic actions.
- [Xia et al., reciprocal recommendations for online dating (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06247) frames recommendations as involving both people's interests. It motivates current reciprocal checks and the proposed readiness/debrief loop, not a claim that this app predicts attraction or relationship success.
- [Pronk and Denissen, choice overload in online dating (2020)](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/a-rejection-mind-set-choice-overload-in-online-dating/) found increasingly rejecting behavior across three online dating studies. It motivates testing smaller batches, but does not establish a particular batch size or guarantee the same effect in a human-led matchmaking service.

## A practical pilot

Ask a small set of matchmakers to perform comparable client-review tasks with their current process and with the assistant; alternate task order. Measure time to a reviewed shortlist, missed deal-breakers, unsupported AI claims, and unresolved data gaps. Collect client response and mutual second-meeting interest separately over time. Keep cohort size and definitions visible; do not treat a small observational pilot as causal proof or optimize acceptance at the expense of client comfort.

## Verification update

The configured Gemini key was tested with a real structured response and a real client-scoped assistant answer on synthetic data. A live check exposed an output-format incompatibility; the shared adapter now uses `responseMimeType` plus `responseJsonSchema`, verified against Gemini 2.5 Flash. One full request encountered a temporary provider error; a subsequent request succeeded. The UI retains explicit retry and guided fallback. This confirms integration behavior, not a general accuracy rate or guaranteed provider availability.

Production build and 31 unit tests passed. Assistant browser tests cover evidence display, exact-profile navigation, client switching, unchanged client records, error recovery and invalid requests. All seven routes were checked at desktop, tablet and mobile widths without browser errors or horizontal overflow.
